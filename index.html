Now building: TeeSniper

A tee-time assistant for golfers. You tell it when and where you want to play. It watches availability around the clock and alerts you the moment a matching tee time or deal appears, so booking happens while the slot still exists.

Creating a watch rule

Saved preferences, not saved searches

Courses, days, tee-off window, party size and budget become a standing watch rule with an expiry date. No daily re-searching.

Alerts measured in seconds

Cancellations and new hot deals surface the moment they are listed. The alert links straight to the booking page.

Booking through official channels

Reservations complete on the marketplace that lists the tee time, under its terms. We bring the demand; the booking stays where it belongs.

Built for real scarcity

Weekend morning slots in a busy metro disappear in minutes. TeeSniper exists so a golfer who will actually play the round gets there first.

Working prototype in private pilot. Launching in Los Angeles and Orange County.

How we integrate

Products that sit on top of marketplaces only work long-term when the marketplace is better off for it. These rules are written into our code, not just our copy.

Official channels first

Where a partner or affiliate API exists, we use it, and every booking completes on the platform that lists the inventory. Our job is converting intent into completed reservations for the marketplace and its courses.

Respectful request volumes

Availability is cached so one query serves every user watching the same course. Rate limits slow our scanning down; they are never retried against.

One golfer, one round

No inventory hoarding and no resale, by design. A watch rule secures at most one tee time, for one person, who intends to play it.

Verified before automated

Nothing books automatically until the integration is verified end to end. Release gates in the codebase stay closed until that evidence exists.
